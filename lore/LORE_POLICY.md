# Lore policy — history rather than a single static wiki page

The LLM must distinguish **world fact**, **in-world claim**, **contested/legendary report**, **campaign divergence** and **GM invention**.

## Each lore entry should eventually record
- `id`, `title`, `entry_type` (place/faction/person/event/institution/practice)
- `valid_from`, `valid_to` (with era and uncertainty; don't invent precise dates)
- `regions`, `actors`, `related_entries`
- `claims`: summary in original prose, `confidence`, `perspective`, `sources` (links/titles), `contradicts` if relevant
- `gm_notes`: content warning / continuity risks, not campaign secrets.

## Chronology and contradiction
- Start with player-selected era, date and region. Don't place later institutions, technologies, wars or rulers into an earlier campaign without a documented divergence.
- In-world propaganda, myth, Dragon Break accounts and unreliable narrators may disagree; preserve contradictions with perspective and source, not a false single answer.
- Use original summaries and links to authoritative/primary in-game evidence where possible; never bulk-copy unofficial wiki prose, books, art, dialogue or maps.
- Changes caused by an individual PC belong to **that private campaign's world state**, not to this public reference repository.
- When evidence is thin, mark it uncertain and let the GM make explicit, locally consistent inventions; no retrospective retcon of already established secret truths.

## Spoilers
Reusable public reference lore may expose facts about published games. Private generated mysteries, villain plans and unrevealed campaign twists must never be committed here.
