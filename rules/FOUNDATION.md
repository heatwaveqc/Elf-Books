# Classless d20 adaptation — design boundaries

This is a **design brief**, not a finished ruleset.

We keep transparent d20 resolution, familiar six ability scores **as an option**, attack/defense vocabulary, conditions, armor and saves where they can be reconciled. A d20 die does not oblige the game to retain classes, levels, spells-per-day or per-level HP gains.

## Dependencies to resolve before play
1. **Capability representation:** Is a skill a 0–100 TES score, a small d20 bonus, or both? Define the translation, including untrained penalties and skill caps.
2. **Check:** `d20 + ability contribution + skill contribution + situational modifiers vs DC`; decide coefficients and ensure achievable DCs over the whole career.
3. **Resistance:** saving throws, opponent contests, spell resistance and reactive defenses must all use the same capability scale.
4. **Survivability:** level-less HP, fatigue/resources, weapon damage, armor and wounds. Prevent an experienced smith from becoming much tougher solely because they practiced crafting.
5. **Combat:** attack and defense skill improvements, initiative, action economy, armor interaction, multiattack and encounter threat based on actual capabilities rather than assumed party level.
6. **Magic:** Magicka, schools, magnitude/duration/area/range costs, learning and spell crafting; no assumed slots or class-based spell access.
7. **Equipment/economy:** era-appropriate gear, enchanting, soul gems, alchemy and scarcity rather than universal market catalogs.
8. **Advancement:** actions that count as practice; probability or progress budgets; caps; what changes abilities, HP, Magicka and special perks.

## Optional archetypes
An archetype is a **starting allocation**, a label and perhaps a set of personal hooks. Choosing “Battlemage” never gates stealth training, alchemy or weapon proficiencies. If backgrounds convey discounts or bonuses, those advantages must be accounted for equitably and do not grant arbitrary extra long-term power.

## Balance principle
Evaluate both low and high skill extremes against DC and opponent distributions. A +1 on a d20 is +5 percentage points in a normal DC window: a TES 1-point gain should **not** automatically add +1 to d20 checks. Test whether different growth policies produce characters of comparable capability after equal meaningful adventures.
